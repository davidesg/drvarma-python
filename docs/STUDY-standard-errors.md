# Standard errors: BFGS or fdhess — the study

*2026-09-27. Data: [`STUDY-standard-errors-data.md`](STUDY-standard-errors-data.md),
generated by `bench/se_study.py` (about 40 s; `--quick` for a short run).
Pins: `tests/test_stderr.py`.*

## The question

The covariance of the estimates is `2 F H⁻¹ / n` (drvmlest.c `est` [3]). Two
choices of `H`:

* **BFGS**: the Hessian `raxopt` accumulated during the search. This has been
  the default in the C since the 1990s.
* **fdhess**: Mauricio's finite-difference Hessian *at the optimum* (Dennis &
  Schnabel A5.6.2). It is part of the published code, but it was left commented
  out in fue and drvarma; drtran enabled it. Here it comes from
  `_qnewt.fdhess`, the faithful port (not fue's `_fdhess`, see fue BUG-0015).

The comparison uses independent references: the exact GLS, OLS, and the
Monte Carlo dispersion. The default is chosen by those results, not by
agreement between the two methods.

## Results

| test | reference | BFGS | fdhess |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 exact GLS (μ + 11 deterministic terms + AR(1)) | closed-form GLS | off by up to **1483 %**, and different from each start | within **0.35 %**, from either start |
| S2 the review's IPC/WTI VAR(1) | OLS | φ21 SE 0.027 against 2.43 (t = −99) | fd/OLS **0.989** for all four (the ML n vs OLS n−k factor is 0.993) |
| S3 VAR(1), scale ratio 1…300, 20 seeds | OLS | collapses from ratio 30 on (worst 0.001) | median 0.99–1.00 at every ratio; one seed at ratio 300 gave 1.88 |
| S4 Monte Carlo, 200 reps: VAR(1) at ratio 30, VARMA(1,1), ARMA(1,1) | sd of the estimates | coverage 0.87–0.95 | coverage 0.90–0.96, at least BFGS's in 13 of 14 coefficients |
| S5 12 real cases (ladder and `.inp`) | — | — | positive definite in 11; 1 on the boundary, where BFGS is kept and the report says so; extra cost 0–0.4 s |

Reading:

1. **BFGS standard errors are not a property of the model.** They depend on the
   path of the search (S1: two starts give two sets of standard errors). They
   fail in the directions that matter: the flattest ones, which carry the
   largest standard errors, and those of series on very different scales
   (S2, S3). A start at the optimum, which is how a `.pre` is run, gives the
   search almost nothing to accumulate. This is why the `.pre` row in S1 is
   still wrong by a factor of 2.3.
2. **fdhess measures the curvature at the optimum.** It matches every
   independent reference within numerical precision. In the Monte Carlo (S4)
   the gain is concentrated where BFGS is known to fail. In the VAR at scale
   ratio 30, the cross coefficient's SE/sd is 0.98 for fdhess and 0.90 for
   BFGS, and its coverage 0.94 against 0.87. In the well-identified VARMA(1,1)
   and ARMA(1,1) both methods are close, within 1–3 %. There, fdhess is
   slightly larger and its coverage is equal or better. It is never worse by
   more than one replication in 200. The one bad seed in S3 is at a scale
   ratio of 300, beyond any real case.
3. **When the Monte Carlo is badly identified, neither method is right.** A
   first VARMA(1,1) design had near-common AR and MA factors. There the
   estimates spread over ±4, both methods undercovered, and the fdhess Hessian
   was not positive definite in 12 of 40 replications. The not-PD guard is the
   useful signal in that case: it says there is no curvature to invert.
   Undercoverage from BFGS says nothing. The design was replaced by a
   well-identified one; the lesson is kept here.

## Decision

**fdhess is the default in both paths** (the ladder and the `.inp` path), and
`hessian="bfgs"` / `-hessian bfgs` is kept for comparison with old outputs.
The engine never falls back silently. The method in use is stated in every
report (`Standard errors: …`) and in `export_fit` (`std_error_method`). When
the Hessian cannot be used, BFGS is kept and the report says why:

* `bfgs (fdhess: the optimum is on the boundary of the admissible region)`: a
  neighbour of the optimum is non-invertible, non-stationary, or has a Q that
  is not positive definite. No unrestricted Hessian exists there (drvec
  BUG-34/49).
* `bfgs (fdhess: the Hessian is not positive definite)`: the check is a plain
  Cholesky. The C's `choldcp` is a modified Cholesky that patches pivots and
  would hide this case.

### The flat direction (`.inp` path)

The likelihood concentrates σ², so it is exactly invariant to `Q → cQ`, and
the Hessian is singular along that direction. The ladder removes the direction
by construction (`Q11 = 1`). In the `.inp` path, `qq[1,1]` is held at its
optimum while the Hessian is taken. Its standard error is then reported as
`(normalised)` (NaN / JSON null): on its own it is not identified. The other
standard errors are conditional on it. That is the right quantity for every
ratio of Q, and for every AR/MA coefficient, because those are invariant.

## The C engines (atsw-gui, same day)

drvarma C now has `-hessian fd|bfgs`, with fd as the default in both paths.
It uses the same guards: `qq[1,1]` held in the `.inp` path, a plain-Cholesky
check, and the boundary sentinel. drtran C gets the guards. In both engines
the `choldcp` status used to be overwritten after the call, so a failed
factorisation was lost.

* **drvarma's bench** (`tests/banco`, 18 cases). The estimates are
  identical and only SE, t, p and Wald move. One Wald conclusion changes
  (c1: p 0.0425 → 0.0676). Three cases whose MA has roots at modulus 1.00005
  keep BFGS, flagged as boundary.
* **drtran's battery** §3d. At the omega₀/σ₁₂ ridge the Hessian is not
  positive definite. The old modified Cholesky had patched it into
  |t| > 100, and the battery pinned that artefact as the pathology. It now
  says the Hessian is not positive definite (drtran BUG-56).

## When there is no BFGS Hessian either

`raxopt` starts its factor at the identity. A search that stops at step 0 has
built nothing, and the fallback would report sqrt(2F/n) for every parameter.
That is the normal case when a `.pre` already is the optimum. So the fallback
applies only if the search iterated. Otherwise there are no standard errors
(NaN) and the method reads `none (fdhess: <why>; the search did not move, so
it built no BFGS Hessian)`. The rule is the same in drvarma C, drtran C,
drtran-python (aligned the same day: the boundary check and the fallback, BUG-56)
and here.

## Why BFGS was the default

Every legacy copy of `drvmlest.c`, from drvus 1.0 to the m6 (DAG) programs of
2003, carries the same line: "This is an alternative way of computing the
second derivative matrix", followed by the commented-out `fdhess` and `choldcp`
calls. The sources give no reason, and neither do the readme, ChangeLog or
todo files. Two reasons are plausible, and neither is documented:

* **Cost.** `fdhess` takes (k² + 3k)/2 likelihood evaluations. For m6's 55
  parameters that is 1595 evaluations, each an exact VARMA likelihood.
  BFGS costs nothing extra. Today that is 0–0.4 s on the real cases (S5).
* **It always answers.** The BFGS factor is positive definite by
  construction. `fdhess` followed by a modified Cholesky may not be positive
  definite, and it can fail at a boundary. Without the guards above, that
  gives numbers that look fine and mean nothing.
