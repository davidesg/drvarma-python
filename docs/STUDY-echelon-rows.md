# Echelon stage 2: row degrees without the restriction table — the study

*2026-10-11. Stage 2 of [`ECHELON_PLAN.md`](ECHELON_PLAN.md) §1. Data:
[`STUDY-echelon-rows-data.md`](STUDY-echelon-rows-data.md), generated by
`research/echelon_seed/fits.py` and `fits_analyze.py`.*

## The question

`Ladder(rows=)` gives each equation its own cross orders, i.e. the row
degrees of the echelon form, without its restriction table and without A₀.
This study measures how exact ML behaves on it, against the unrestricted
VARMA(N, N), on systems whose Kronecker indices are known. The result is the
baseline that stage 3 has to beat.

## Design

- The 9 systems of stage 0, in both designs (generic, and weak with the cross
  coefficients × 0.25). nobs 200, 50 replications, the same seeds as stage 0.
- Each replication goes through the Ladder on route V's specifications:
  - each series is a fue `.inp` with its mean and a free own ARMA;
  - **full**: own AR(N)/MA(N), cross (N, N) on every pair, N = max n_i;
  - **rows**: own AR(n_i)/MA(n_i), cross (n_i, n_i) on row i.
- `rows` is nested in `full`, so their LR is a test of `rows`.

## Results

1. **With equal indices, the two forms are the same model.** That holds for
   (1,1), (2,2) and (1,1,1): an echelon form with equal indices is the
   standard VARMA(n, n), with A₀ = I and nothing restricted. Their rows in the
   data coincide, and they are the clean baseline:
   - generic design: termcode 3 in 0–16 % of the fits, never on the MA wall;
   - **weak design: termcode 3 in 22–54 %, on the wall in 16–50 %.**

   So weak cross dynamics alone produce the flat directions, even in an
   identified form. The echelon table will not remove those stops. §8's
   prediction has to be read against this baseline, not against zero.
2. **With unequal indices, `rows` is misspecified, as expected.** The LR
   against `full` rejects at 5 % in:
   - generic design: 100 % of (1,0), 84 % of (2,1), 98 % of (2,1,0), 86 % of
     (2,2,1);
   - weak design: 30–70 %.

   The row degrees in the standard form are not the n_i: A₀⁻¹ mixes the rows,
   so a row of low index inherits the lags of a higher one. The fix is A₀, the
   contemporaneous terms that stage 3 adds. Without it the row degrees alone
   are not a usable model.
3. **Over-parameterisation is what drives the MA to the wall.** With
   unequal indices, `full` ends with an MA root on the invertibility
   boundary in 28–96 % of the fits ((1,2), (1,2,1), (2,1,0), (2,2,1)), and in
   more than half of them except weak (1,2). `rows`, with fewer parameters,
   does so in 2–36 %. This is the
   Lütkepohl–Poskitt symptom, "the estimated MA operator has a tendency to
   converge to the boundary", measured.
4. **The `full` search often does not find its own maximum.** In up to 20 %
   of the replications its logL is BELOW that of `rows`, a model nested in
   it. That is the flat likelihood of an unidentified form, and the optimiser
   stops wherever its path ends.
5. **termcode 3 is a weak signal on its own.** It is frequent in both forms,
   and in this optimiser it also ends searches that sit at an optimum
   (BUG-0010). The MA wall and the negative LR are the readable symptoms.
   Stage 3 will report all three.
6. **Speed:** `rows` takes 40–75 % of `full`'s time on the three-series
   systems (e.g. 5 s against 12 s for (2,1,0) generic).

## What it found in the package

The study ran 1,800 fits through the Ladder and turned up three defects:

| bug | what | state |
|---|---|---|
| BUG-0017 | the LR against the diagonal counted (p + q) per pair whatever `zeros` fixed | fixed |
| BUG-0018 | an overflowing log(Q_ii/Q_11) in the line search raised out of the fit | fixed |
| BUG-0019 | a singular fdhess Hessian (AR1 and MA1 of a white-noise regressor, the same coefficient) raised out of the fit | fixed; the case is rerun in the data |
| BUG-0020 | the diagonal gate fails with NaN when a univariate optimum sits on the MA wall (root 0.99995): admissible alone, ifault 3 in the joint elf | open; the one replication ((2,2,1) generic, rep 45) is left out |

## Decision

- **`rows=` ships as a tool, not as a form to fit.** It is what `zeros`
  already was, per equation, and stage 3 builds on it.
- **Stage 3 must add A₀** (plan §2.1, unchanged). Measure 2 is the evidence.
- **Stage 3's yardstick is this table.** For each system, the echelon form
  is fitted on the same data and compared with `full` and `rows` on four
  things:
  - the LR against `full`, which should not reject beyond its size;
  - the wall;
  - logL below a nested model's;
  - time.
- The equal-index systems keep their stops in the weak design whatever the
  form. They measure the weak identification of the cross dynamics, not
  over-parameterisation.
